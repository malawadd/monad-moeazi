## What the evidence does not establish

- Different providers, routes, prompts, or model aliases are not proof of independent
  analytical errors.
- Correlation is not causation; agreement can be correct and disagreement can be noise.
- Negative-correlation learning does not validate rewarding contrarian LLM output.
- No production study shows two model families reduce crypto forecast error or PnL.

## Counterevidence and data limits

- Current settled outcomes are synthesis-level rather than mature specialist-path
  forecasts; freeform summaries cannot be assigned a valid residual.
- Market outcomes are serially dependent, overlapping, sparse, delayed, and
  nonstationary. Model, prompt, evidence, and route versions drift.
- Shared evidence can dominate nominal model diversity, while clustering may miss a
  copied or derived source.
- Simpson's paradox can reverse aggregate conclusions across markets or regimes.
- Maximizing disagreement can reduce accuracy and increase cost or abstention.

## Freshness

every cohort. An upstream alias change invalidates incompatible comparisons.
