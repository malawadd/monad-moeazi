# The Next Wave of Arbitrum Users Will Not Start With a Trading Terminal

*Arbitrum’s financial infrastructure is expanding. The next distribution challenge is to make that infrastructure meaningful to people who do not already speak the language of markets.*

The crypto industry has spent years making onchain markets deeper, faster, and more expressive. Yet the dominant entrance still assumes that users arrive knowing what to buy, where to trade, which network to choose, how to fund a wallet, and how to judge risk.

That assumption limits the market.

We believe a large future audience for Arbitrum will not begin with a trading terminal at all. They will begin with a familiar mobile experience, an understandable financial goal, and assistance that helps them participate within boundaries they can see.

Moeazi Mobile is our attempt to build that entrance.

## The market is moving toward consumer and agentic finance

Recent developments make the direction increasingly visible.

[Robinhood Chain launched its public mainnet in July 2026](https://blog.arbitrum.io/robinhood-chain-mainnet/), built with the Arbitrum Platform and positioned around consumer-scale access to tokenized real-world assets. Robinhood’s launch materials describe an ecosystem spanning [Stock Tokens, spot markets, lending, perpetuals, and agentic crypto accounts](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-accelerates-global-expansion-robinhood-chain-mainnet-stock-tokens-agentic-trading/). Its current ecosystem documentation includes infrastructure across [exchange, lending, stablecoins, market data, custody, and compliance](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/).

The behavioral signal is also notable. Robinhood reported [nearly 100,000 Agentic Trading accounts with more than $100 million in assets under custody at the end of Q2 2026](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-second-quarter-2026-results). That does not prove demand for Moeazi, nor does it guarantee broader adoption. It does suggest that delegated, bounded financial assistance is becoming a category users can recognize.

For us, the lesson is larger than any one product: programmable finance is moving toward mainstream interfaces, user-defined guardrails, and software that can act on behalf of people.

The access layer now matters as much as the market layer.

## Beginner-first distribution is ecosystem infrastructure

New users do not create ecosystem value simply because an app makes a transaction easy. Durable participation begins when people understand what they own, what they authorized, and how to remain in control.

That is why we think trust and simplicity are adoption infrastructure.

A beginner-first gateway can help a new participant:

- establish an identity and own an Arbitrum wallet;
- fund it through a guided experience;
- choose an understandable goal;
- place only a bounded amount under management;
- observe AI-assisted analysis and activity;
- pause, stop, and review at any time;
- learn enough to explore more of the ecosystem deliberately.

Each successful step can become a bridge to deeper participation. A person who begins by understanding a stablecoin balance may later become curious about lending. Someone who learns to review a bounded strategy may later want to inspect venues, yield products, or more advanced risk controls. A user who starts on mobile may eventually open the Moeazi web control room to configure the system directly.

This is a potential adoption flywheel, not a claim of proven conversion. It is a thesis we intend to test with measurable user understanding and real behavior.

## Moeazi can complement the markets Arbitrum is creating

Arbitrum’s opportunity is becoming broader than a single chain or product category. The Arbitrum Foundation described the first half of 2026 as an [“institutional moment” for the ecosystem](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/the-arbitrum-foundation-h1-2026-progress-update/31378), citing expansion by financial and consumer brands. Work toward [ZK settlement](https://blog.arbitrum.io/zk-settlement-is-coming/) also points toward faster finality and more capital-efficient infrastructure across the Arbitrum Platform.

Moeazi’s role would be complementary: help users translate intent into understandable access to the financial primitives emerging across that platform.

We are Arbitrum One-first. It is the active strategy network around which we are building and validating our initial system. Over time—and only after the necessary technical, safety, eligibility, and product validation—we see a wider opportunity to help users navigate relevant markets across the Arbitrum Platform.

Moeazi has no affiliation with, endorsement from, or current integration with Robinhood or Robinhood Chain. We reference Robinhood Chain and Stock Tokens because they are important evidence of where programmable finance is heading, not because we are claiming a partnership.

## The mobile app is the front door, not the whole building

The first experience should be approachable. The underlying system should not be shallow.

Moeazi Mobile is designed to give a new participant clarity, bounded assistance, and visible control. The web app is designed for the participant who wants to go deeper: choosing models, configuring specialist agents, inspecting evidence, defining mandates, setting authority modes, approving actions, comparing venues, and reviewing costs and execution.

Both surfaces connect to the same larger ambition: an access, intelligence, and accountability layer for programmable finance.

That continuity matters to investors and ecosystem partners. It creates the possibility of serving a user before they know how to trade and continuing to serve them as their knowledge, capital, and needs become more sophisticated.

## What we intend to prove

The strongest version of this thesis will not be validated by downloads alone. We want to learn whether:

- first-time users can successfully reach an owned and funded Arbitrum wallet;
- they can identify the amount placed inside a managed boundary;
- they understand their limits, permissions, and stop controls;
- visible analysis helps them learn rather than simply follow;
- they return to review markets and activity;
- they progress from beginner guidance to more advanced participation;
- certified activity eventually creates useful demand for Arbitrum venues and applications.

These are hypotheses, and they deserve disciplined measurement.

## A larger front door for Arbitrum

The next wave of users may not identify as traders. They may not want to operate a terminal. They may simply want a credible way to understand what programmable finance can do for them—and a system that respects how much they do not yet know.

We believe Arbitrum can support the markets of that future. Moeazi Mobile can help make them reachable.

Our ambition is to bring non-traders into the ecosystem carefully: not by erasing complexity, but by translating it; not by demanding blind trust, but by creating visible boundaries; and not by trapping beginners in a simplified product, but by giving them a path toward mastery.

That is the distribution opportunity we are building toward. We invite Arbitrum teams, wallets, protocols, researchers, and long-term partners to build it with us.
